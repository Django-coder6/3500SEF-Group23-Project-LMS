14| ## 1. What this log records
15| 
16| Any change to scope, requirements or design is recorded here before it is
17| carried out. A change that is not in this log did not happen.
18| 
19| ### 1.1 The change process
20| 
21| The process has three stages. It follows the change management process
22| described in the course material on requirements engineering.
23| 
24| | Stage | What happens | Who does it |
25| |---|---|---|
26| | 1. Problem analysis and change specification | The request is written down as a change record: what is wrong or missing, and what the requester wants instead. | The person who raises it |
27| | 2. Change analysis and cost | The effect on requirements, design, code, tests and the schedule is worked out. The cost is stated in hours and in risk. | R2 for requirement and scope effect, R6 or R4 for technical effect, R1 for schedule effect |
28| | 3. Change implementation | The change is applied to every affected artefact, and the record is closed with a reference to the pull requests that carried it out. | The owner named in the record |
29| 
30| A change is only started once stage 2 is complete. If the analysis shows the
31| change would not fit the remaining schedule, the record is closed as rejected,
32| with the reason stated.
33| 
34| ### 1.2 Change record format
35| 
36| | Field | Meaning |
37| |---|---|
38| | Change ID | CR-nnn, numbered in order |
39| | Date | When the record was opened |
40| | Type | Requirement, design, scope, or process |
41| | Item affected | The requirement ID, design document or plan entry |
42| | Before | What the document said before the change |
43| | Reason | Why the change is needed, with the evidence |
44| | After | What it will say after the change |
45| | Impact | High, medium or low, and which artefacts are affected |
46| | Raised by | The member who raised it |
47| | Implementing sprint | Which sprint carries the work |
48| | Status | Proposed, approved, rejected, implemented or closed |
49| 
50| ### 1.3 How a record number is taken
51| 
52| **A number is taken when it appears in the table in section 2**, not when it is mentioned in an
53| issue, a comment or a review.
54| 
55| A number written into an issue is a *reservation*: a suggestion to whoever writes next. It is
56| courteous to honour it, and this team has honoured it every time it noticed, but **the table is what
57| decides**. Three collisions have already come from treating a reservation as a claim — `CR-018`,
58| `CR-026` and `CR-027` — and each was resolved the same way: the record that exists keeps the number
59| and the reservation moves.
60| 
61| **So when two records reach for the same number**, the one already in the table keeps it. The other
62| takes the next free one, and the issue or comment holding the reservation is edited to say so.
